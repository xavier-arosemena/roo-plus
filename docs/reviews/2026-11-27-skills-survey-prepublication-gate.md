The six cross-repository pairs are exactly the six named, path for path: `brand-guidelines`, `internal-comms`, `template-skill`↔`template`, `webapp-testing`, `theme-factory`, `canvas-design`. **Does the new conclusion survive?** Yes, and it is the strongest thing in the report. NVIDIA 281,621 vs Composio 94,430 = **2.98×** on 223 _fewer_ skills; per-skill description cost is 439 (NVIDIA), 109 (Composio), 558 (google) — a 5× spread at fixed inventory logic. "Cost tracks publisher style, not inventory size" is what the data says. "Composio has the most skills and the third-highest metadata cost" also holds (third behind NVIDIA and google). "Fourteen times the body budget" holds (70,404 ÷ 5,000 = 14.1).

**What fails — A6, the `measurable` column.** The column is not parsed coverage; it is the single-line coverage of the draft this table replaced. NVIDIA 361 = 641 − 280 folded; google 10 = 226 − 216; microsoft 123 = 215 − 92; K-Dense 174 = 177 − 3; MengTo 223 = 223 − 0. A reader who trusts the honesty column will under-estimate the table's coverage and still miss the one row that is actually short.

**A7, the unshown rows.** "anthropics ~1,600 tokens, obra ~580, slavingia ~490" — obra (2,317 → 579) and slavingia (1,950 → 488) are parsed-and-text-only-identical because neither has folded descriptions. anthropics does: 3 folded rows, parsed total **9,452 characters = ~2,363 tokens**, not ~1,600. The ~1,600 is 6,421 ÷ 4 — the _text-only_ sum from the previous draft. So three of the "small catalogues not shown" are quoted in the retired convention, inside a table whose preamble says "Measured on **parsed frontmatter** … every row counts the text a client would actually receive".

**Detail, low severity.** MengTo's "≥19,400" tokens: ≥77,573 characters ÷ 4 = ≥19,393, so the floor of the token estimate is 19,393; "≥19,400" rounds a floor upward, which is the one direction a floor must not move. Say "≥19,300" or express the floor in characters.

(b) Mark the corpus total **≥824,853** (or recover MengTo's 13 unparsed descriptions) and say why. (c) anthropics → "~2,400 tokens". (d) "≥19,400" → "≥19,300". (e) State the newline normalisation once, if exact character totals are to be quoted to the unit.

The published set is `don't use for`, `do not use for`, `not intended for`, `do not trigger`, `out of scope` added to the base six. That set, applied where detector 2 is applied (the `SKILL.md`), returns **428**, not 442. I could not reach 442 by adding phrases (the nearest neighbours are 431, 432, 433), by widening the scope (495, 552), or by using curly apostrophes. The number sits outside every definition I could construct; the closest defensible statement is "≈428 (16.1%)", and it is still a lower bound. Note also that the _previous_ review's own composition for 442 did not add up (90 + 407 claimed new hits ≠ 442), so 442 may have been carried across as a figure rather than recomputed — which is precisely the failure mode §2.8 says was repaired.

**A2 — the attribution is wrong.** The table row reads "The specification's machine-readable field (`not-for:`)" and line 501 repeats "the one machine-readable slot the specification offers". The specification at `agentskills/agentskills@83021e2be457:docs/specification.mdx` (247 lines) permits `name`, `description`, `license`, `compatibility`, `metadata`, `allowed-tools` — I grepped the whole clone for `not-for` and `not_for` and got **zero** hits. `not-for:` is a NVIDIA extension, which §4.2 of this same report correctly classifies as a non-specification field and which the reference validator would reject. So §7.2 contradicts §4.1, §4.2 and the spec itself, and does so in the one table whose job is to distinguish machine-readable from prose. That inversion is not minor: "the specification offers a slot and only 3 files use it" is a finding about the standard; "one vendor invented a field and 3 files use it" is a finding about adoption. The second is what the data supports.

**A3 — the unmatched example.** §7.2's third quotation is "Skip it when the user asked a trivial how-to or simple lookup… Do this at most once per conversation." It comes from `anthropics/skills/skills/discernment-nudge/SKILL.md` (line 12, in the folded description). That file matches **none** of the six base phrases and **none** of the five expanded ones. The previous review blocked on exactly this defect for the "Do NOT use for" example; the rewrite replaced that example and reproduced the defect with a different one. Two of the four examples are fine now (the Google `Don't use for` and the `Do NOT use for:` quotes match the expanded set; `not-for:` matches detector 1), and one is outside the measurement it illustrates.

**Does the conclusion survive at 16.1%?** Yes — more strongly than at 16.7%. The three rates are 0.1%, 3.4% and ≥16.1%, against 6 of 22 collections that test behaviour at all (§6.4). "Over-firing is the least-defended failure mode" does not depend on the third digit, and the §7.2 example mismatch is itself evidence for the claim: the report's own best exemplar of a stated boundary would not be counted by its own detector. ### 4.2 What fails

**B1 — "Nineteen of 22 collections carry a permissive licence in-tree" ([§6.5](../research/2026-11-26-agent-skills-field-survey.md:453)). Tests: 1 ✗, 2 ✓, 3 ✓, 4 ~.**

No path, no SHA, no list; the reader must re-clone 22 repositories to check a count. Worse, the count only works if the three exceptions are `openai` (no root licence, per-skill terms), `slavingia` (none) and **`multica-ai/andrej-karpathy-skills`** (no licence file at all) — but the next sentence names a different three for "needs attention": openai, slavingia and **Composio**. A reader will read 22 − 19 = 3 = "the three that need attention", and that is not the set. And the classification is materially kind to the largest catalogue: Composio has **no root licence**, 9 per-skill Apache-2.0 files, 4 proprietary, and **0 licence instruments for the 832 generated wrappers**. Counting that as "carries a permissive licence in-tree" because 9 files do is technically true and practically misleading — the kind of sentence the section exists to prevent.

_Minimal fix:_ drop the count and keep the mechanism — "Most collections ship a permissive licence in-tree; the inventory records it per repository. Three do not: no root licence in `openai/skills`, none at all in `slavingia/skills` and `multica-ai/andrej-karpathy-skills`. Composio ships a permissive licence per skill for 9 of its 864 files, a proprietary notice on four, and none on the rest." That is longer, checkable, and puts the emphasis on the mechanism (a field can carry a stale or absent notice) rather than on a tally.

**B2 — "every one a `ComposioHQ/awesome-claude-skills` file copied verbatim from `anthropics/skills`" ([§3.6](../research/2026-11-26-agent-skills-field-survey.md:244)). Tests: 1 ✓, 2 ✗, 3 ✓, 4 ~.**

The hashes establish byte-identity and nothing else. "Copied verbatim from" states an act and a direction by a named party. The inference is almost certainly right — the six skills are Anthropic's own, and §6.5's retained © notices corroborate — but the sentence converts an inference into a finding, in the section most likely to be read by a lawyer. It is also the sentence that most directly contradicts §6.5's "not an assertion about any party's conduct or intent" (B4).

_Minimal fix, one word-class:_ "…and **6 groups that span two repositories** — in each, a `ComposioHQ/awesome-claude-skills` file is **byte-identical to** one in `anthropics/skills`: …". The finding is unchanged; the assertion of conduct is removed. If direction is wanted, state it as inference: "the direction is indicated by the retained Anthropic notice, not by the hash".

**B3 — "A derivative carrying the original's proprietary notice" and "the same skills in the original publisher's repository ship Apache-2.0" ([§6.5](../research/2026-11-26-agent-skills-field-survey.md:459)). Tests: 1 ~, 2 ~, 3 ✓, 4 ✗.**

Three problems in one row. (i) The title asserts a relationship ("a derivative") that the section supports only circumstantially — the table row is where a reader stops, and the basis for the inference appears 4 lines later. (ii) "carry `license: Proprietary` with `© 2025 Anthropic, PBC. All rights reserved.`" attaches the © line to the _frontmatter field_; the field actually says "Proprietary. LICENSE.txt has complete terms" and the © is in the sibling `LICENSE.txt`. A publisher could fairly object that the report says its frontmatter carries a notice it does not contain — a small inaccuracy, in the one table where small inaccuracies are expensive. (iii) "the same skills" asserts identity across two repositories whose files differ substantially (Composio's `docx/SKILL.md` is 197 lines with a rewritten body; Anthropic's is 92), so the honest word is "same-named".

The row is also the only place where the emphasis falls on the party rather than the mechanism — the very next paragraph gets this right ("a `license:` field is a claim by the republisher, not a grant by the author").

_Minimal fix:_ retitle the row as an observation about files — "**A per-skill `LICENSE.txt` whose terms forbid distribution, shipping beside skills whose same-named originals are Apache-2.0**" — and correct the field/notice conflation: "carry `license: Proprietary` and a `LICENSE.txt` headed `© 2025 Anthropic, PBC. All rights reserved.`". Both changes keep every fact and remove the two over-assertions.

**B4 — "not legal advice and not an assertion about any party's conduct or intent" ([§6.5](../research/2026-11-26-agent-skills-field-survey.md:467)). Tests: 1 ✓, 2 ~, 3 ✓, 4 ✓.**

_Minimal fix:_ either apply B2 and B3 (then the sentence becomes true as written), or narrow it — "not legal advice, and no allegation of wrongdoing against any party".

**B5 — "Ask the publisher; until then treat the material as source-only" ([§8](../research/2026-11-26-agent-skills-field-survey.md:558)). Tests: 1 ✓, 2 ✓, 3 ✗, 4 ✓.**

"Treat the material as source-only" is the report telling a reader what they may do with someone else's code — the one place the document crosses from describing to advising. It is prudent advice, and that is the problem: prudence is not the report's role, and a reader may rely on it.

_Minimal fix:_ "The files record no grant, so the position cannot be resolved from the tree; only the publisher can say." Same warning, no instruction.

**B6 — "One vendor ships 9 distinct per-skill `LICENSE.txt` files" ([§6.5](../research/2026-11-26-agent-skills-field-survey.md:457)). Tests: 1 ✓, 2 ✓, 3 ✓, 4 ~.**

"Distinct" is a byte count. The nine contents are: several Apache-2.0 files that differ only in whitespace/line breaks, one MIT, one Notion copyright notice, and the Figma terms. Calling that "9 distinct licences" overstates the variety of terms in a way that flatters the finding. _Minimal fix:_ "9 distinct licence **files** (Apache-2.0 formatting variants, MIT, a Notion copyright notice, and Figma Developer Terms that reserve the right to modify, suspend or discontinue the feature)".

### 4.3 Residual exposure if published as-is under a company name

The most likely complaint would come from the largest named catalogue, and its shape would be: _"your report says we copied Anthropic's files verbatim and that we relicense proprietary material — you assert conduct you cannot see in a hash."_ It would be answered by producing the hashes, not by producing the direction of copying, and the report currently states the direction as fact. It would be strongest against §3.6's "copied verbatim" and §6.5's "A derivative carrying…", and it would be helped by the table's field/notice conflation.

**The one-sentence change that removes it without weakening the finding:** in §6.5, replace "A derivative carrying the original's proprietary notice" with "A per-skill `LICENSE.txt` whose terms forbid distribution, beside same-named skills that are Apache-2.0 upstream", and in §3.6 replace "copied verbatim from" with "byte-identical to". The finding — that a vendoring process inheriting these files inherits a notice that forbids redistribution — survives both edits intact, because it was never about intent.

**What I am not doing.** This is not a legal opinion and must not be read as one. A qualified reader still has to decide: (a) whether the Composio `LICENSE.txt`, which is a verbatim Anthropic notice, imposes obligations on a redistributor of the _edited_ skill directories, and whether the editing changes that; (b) whether "forbids redistribution" is an accurate summary of the "ADDITIONAL RESTRICTIONS" clause under the agreement it references; (c) whether reproducing the © line and the restriction text is permissible quotation; (d) whether naming a commercial publisher in a report published under a company name creates trade-libel or unfair-competition exposure in the relevant jurisdiction, which is a function of the publisher's identity and the commercial relationship, not of the text alone; and (e) whether the report's own redistribution of the corpus material (it quotes and characterises, it does not ship the files) is within bounds. The header already lists the §6.5 legal read as outstanding; nothing in this review substitutes for it.

## 7. Residual risks after the fixes

1. **The legal read still has not happened.** §6.5's facts are right and now locatable, but questions (a)–(e) in §4.3 are for a qualified lawyer, and a report published under a company name carries the publisher's exposure, not the author's. This review reduces the surface; it does not clear it.
2. **MengTo's 13 unparsed descriptions make the corpus total a floor.** Publishing 824,853 as exact invites a reader who recovers those 13 to call the report wrong on a point where it is merely conservative.
3. **The 4-characters-per-token ratio is still unvalidated**, and the report now says so plainly (§5.2). Every token figure in §5.3 and §9.1 inherits that risk; the disclosure is adequate, the estimate is not a measurement, and §1's research question no longer claims otherwise.
4. **§6.1's per-collection cells remain indicative.** The caveat says the two depth readings "differ by about 8%", which is true of the 546 vs 590 total but is a global figure, not a per-row guarantee; the prior review measured `microsoft` at 141 against the table's 90 and could not explain the gap. A reader who recomputes that row will not be reassured by an 8% caveat.
5. **Star counts for seven repositories were transcribed by hand** on 2026-11-27 and carry a transcription risk the API reads do not. The report discloses this (§2.4); a dated dump committed beside the CSV would close it.
6. **§7.2's conclusion is now better supported than its number.** After the fix to 428/16.1%, the sentence "the finding is stronger than the single number suggested" stays true — but only because the detector demonstrably misses its own best example. A future revision that widens the phrase set again should re-run the examples against the new set rather than carrying them forward.
7. **Two decisions are editorial, not factual, and belong to a human:** whether to name the republisher at all in §6.5 (naming makes the claim checkable, which the four tests reward; not naming lowers the exposure), and whether the report keeps the word "derivative" as an inference stated in the evidence paragraph rather than a table-row title. Both are defensible; the current combination is the one that attracts a letter.
