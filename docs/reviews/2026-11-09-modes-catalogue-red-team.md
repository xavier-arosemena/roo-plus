## 1. Verdict

**Stop the plan as written; do not run T-A…T-H in the order given.** Not because the direction is wrong — the comprehension gap and the debugging over-splitting are real, exact, and survive everything I threw at them — but because the plan's headline saving is an accounting transfer rather than a saving, and the two documents that define its unit disagree about what an owner is. Specifically: (a) the plan removes routing cost from the surface that has a gate (`Σ whenToUse`, 12k) and relocates it to a surface with a _smaller_ declared budget and no gate at all (119 skills against a 2,000-char skills index — [`modes-target-state.md`](../modes-target-state.md:315)), so the arithmetic that justifies the restructuring reproduces by construction while the behaviour that matters becomes unmeasured; (b) [`modes-units.md`](../modes-units.md:113) and [`modes-catalogue-brainstorm.md`](../modes-catalogue-brainstorm.md:31) define **21** and **24** coding owners respectively and differ in membership on **11 of 12** axes — only DBG is identical — so T-A/T-B/T-C would each be run against a different object; (c) the cost test T-B is constructed to pass at 150 chars per mode and fails at the design's own 250-char ceiling (§2, O7). Proceed to a bounded revision — reconcile one owner table, specify the skills plane and its budget, restore the failing-eval admission gate, and replace the assimilation instrument with something that can actually produce evidence — then run a narrowed T-A/T-B/T-C with pre-registered pass marks and independent raters. The research programme should continue; this plan should not be tested as if it were one plan.

## 3. The strongest single objection

### The plan does not reduce routing cost; it moves it to a plane with a smaller budget and no gate.

**What the plan claims.** 302 → ~42 owners; preload 90 → ~30; `Σ whenToUse` 13,306 ([`modes-catalogue-brainstorm.md`](../modes-catalogue-brainstorm.md:163)) → the ≈5.7k working figure (5,700 ÷ 150 = 38 modes, so the sizing assumes a 38-mode preload that the plan never names); a saving of roughly 7.6k routing characters.

**What the plan does with the work.** It keeps 119 jobs and turns each into a skill: "Skills carry the job-level procedure (119)" ([`modes-units.md`](../modes-units.md:196)). Skills "inherit the mode's grant and stance" ([`modes-units.md`](../modes-units.md:31)) and are loaded on demand, selected by the model from their descriptions. The same architecture that budgets `whenToUse` at 12k budgets the **skills index at 2,000 characters** ([`modes-target-state.md`](../modes-target-state.md:315)) and allows a single skill description up to 1,024 characters ([`modes-target-state.md`](../modes-target-state.md:405)).

**The arithmetic.** At the shipped mean `whenToUse` length (I measured 147.9 chars across the 90 preload modes), 119 skills cost **119 × 148 ≈ 17,600 chars — 8.8× the skills index budget**. Even at the 40-char gist length used by stage-1 routing, 119 × 40 = **4,760, still 2.4× over**. Under the per-mode reading (skills scoped by `modeSlugs`), the fat owners are worse, not better: the `debug` owner carries 11 skills; at the corpus mean that is **1,628 chars, 81% of the entire index budget for one mode**, and at the permitted 1,024-char description cap it is **11,264 chars, 5.6× over**. The `code` owner is larger still: it absorbs the 12 implementation jobs plus stack skills.

| Ceiling                        | Skill count | Chars  | Against 2,000   | Verdict     |
| ------------------------------ | ----------- | ------ | --------------- | ----------- |
| Corpus mean (147.9)            | 119         | 17,600 | 8.8×            | impossible  |
| 40-char gist                   | 119         | 4,760  | 2.4×            | impossible  |
| Per-mode, `debug` at mean      | 11          | 1,628  | 0.81× of budget | at the edge |
| Per-mode, `debug` at 1,024 cap | 11          | 11,264 | 5.6×            | impossible  |

**Why this is the objection that kills the plan.** The routing budget is the _only_ quantified reason to restructure. If cost is conserved across planes, the plan's primary justification disappears and what remains is a preference about taxonomy. And the two selection surfaces behave differently: `whenToUse` is gated (`gate:budget`, `gate:routing`), measured in CI, and printed on failure; skill selection has no equivalent gate, no eval in T-A…T-H, and a description allowance five times larger than the field it replaces. T-E does not close this: it asks whether eleven debugging skills can be _written_ to cover eleven jobs, which is a statement about the author's ability to write prose, not about whether the model loads the right skill for B3 versus B8 at runtime.

**Failure scenario it implies.**

1. The 42 owners ship; `gate:budget` passes at 8.4k because the authored strings average 200 chars, not 150; the release note says "the list you see is shorter".
2. The two owner tables route the same job differently: a failing third-party integration is an owner (`integration-engineer`) in the brainstorm and a skill under `code` in the units analysis; a privacy question has an owner (`privacy`) in the units analysis and none in the brainstorm, where `security-reviewer` answers it with a threat list instead of S6's field-level data-flow map.
3. Inside `debug`, the model selects among eleven skills from descriptions. Nothing measures whether it picks the benchmark-discipline skill rather than the stack-trace skill. The output is a plausible root cause with no before/after number — exactly the failure the Measure stance was supposed to prevent.
4. `debug`'s doctrine now carries the procedure for eleven jobs, or the skills index for the mode blows its 2k budget and the descriptions are truncated to fit — which degrades the very selection signal the model needs.
5. The migration scorecard reports success: modes ↓, `Σ whenToUse` ↓, skills ↑ ≥ 8. The metric that would have caught the failure — _skill selected correctly ÷ times offered_ — is not in the scorecard, not in the gates, and not in any test.

**What would demote this from FATAL to SERIOUS.** State the skills-index model (global or per-mode), publish a per-skill description budget, add the skills index to the same 12k/2k accounting, and add one eval: for each of the eleven debugging jobs, given the debug mode and its skills, does the model load the right one? If that arithmetic fits and the eval exists, the plan is merely under-specified. Today it is unsupported.

## 5. Where the plan survives

A review that finds only faults is not calibrated. These are the claims I attacked and could not break.

- **The supply numbers are exact and reproducible.** I recomputed from `.roomodes` and `custom-modes/AGENT_CATALOG.md`: 302 catalogue rows, 90 preloaded modes, `Σ whenToUse` = 13,307 (the documents say 13,306/13,307; the one-character difference is a rounding or definition difference, not an error), mean 147.9, median 145.5, p90 177, max 286. When I applied the baseline's own boundary rule (`business-analyst` and `core-web-vitals-seo` are _kept_), the jobless-preload claim reproduced **exactly**: 25 of 90 slots (27.8%) and 3,710 of 13,307 chars (27.9%). That is unusually good practice, and it is why every other number in this review could be checked at all.
- **The comprehension gap survives completely.** I searched all 302 slugs for `explain`, `understand`, `cartograph`, `rationale`, `onboard`: zero hits. The claim "10 jobs, 0 modes, 0 preloaded" is exact, supply-side, and does not depend on the contested job mapping or on the stance vocabulary. It is the strongest finding in the file set.
- **The debugging test survives at job granularity.** Nothing in my attack touches the demonstration that 8 of 11 debugging jobs fail D1 and D2, and that eleven debugging _modes_ would buy ~1,650 routing chars for near-identical grants. That argument is sound. What fails is the _generalisation_ from "jobs → skills under `debug`" to "119 jobs → 119 skills", where the index arithmetic breaks (§3).
- **"Jobs measure need; modes are interventions" is correct and should outlive this plan.** The distinction explains the 302 without moralising about it, and it is the reason the debugging case is decidable at all.
- **The refusal to invent popularity is right.** "No per-mode demand metric exists" is verifiable in-repo and consistent across the research, the architecture and the ADR. The plan does not smuggle a counter in through the back door — it invents a _rubric_ instead, which is a different and smaller sin.
- **The ADR's core decision survives.** Removing the privileged built-in tier, making the default an explicit decision, and requiring every mode to declare a job, a specialisation or a non-coding label are all defensible independent of the owner count. The `BUILT_IN_SLUGS` duplication is a real defect and the plan's fix is the right shape.
- **"Counts are functions, never literals" survives, and it is the reason this review is possible.** Every headline number I checked was recomputable from files. The plan should keep that rule even where it breaks the plan's own claims — which is what §2's O2 and O11 are asking for.

## Appendix — what would make this survivable

The minimum set of changes, in order, with no redesign of the catalogue itself:

1. **Merge the owner tables into one artefact.** One count, one name per owner, one row per job family. Delete or mark the superseded table. Do this before anything else; T-A/T-B/T-C are uninterpretable otherwise.
2. **State the skills plane.** Index model (global vs per-mode), per-skill description budget, expected skills per owner, and the resulting index cost added to the same 12k/2k accounting. If the arithmetic cannot be made to fit, the "119 skills" claim is withdrawn and the unit recommendation is re-derived.
3. **Recompute the routing ceiling three ways** — at 150, at the achieved corpus mean, and at the 250 hard ceiling — and require the design to fit at the last one. A plan that only fits at its most favourable assumption is not a plan.
4. **Restore G2.** No owner is admitted without a named failing eval. Keep an amendment log; a new mode with no failing eval is a skill, not an owner.
5. **Demote D4 from "measured" to "judged"** unless a human instrument (tree test, n ≈ 8–12) is committed. Removing the false precision is worth more than the criterion.
6. **Add T-I, T-J, T-K, T-L and T-M** as listed in §6.2, and pre-register the pass marks in the test document before running anything.
7. **Fix the 302 mapping's error bar.** A second coder on a stratified 60-slug sample; publish the disagreement rate and the corrected range; stop quoting 40.1% to one decimal. The decision does not need the decimal — "roughly a third to two fifths" carries the same weight.
8. **Apply the A21 liability test to A13/A18/A19**, and record the decision that supersedes baseline §5.4 (or drop the axes). A plan cannot exclude legal accountability in its research and add legal owners in its design without saying which one wins.
9. **Decide the preload target and the routing regime once.** 30, 42, 60–90 and 25–40 are four products; stage-1 gist routing changes the marginal cost of every owner by a factor of three. Pick one pair and size the plan against it.

If items 1–3 and 6 are done, the plan becomes testable and the strongest objection becomes a measurement question rather than a refutation. If they are not, the tests will confirm the design for the same reason the design was written: because the authors chose the assumptions the tests run on.
