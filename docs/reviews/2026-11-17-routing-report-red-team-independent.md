## Verdict

**Revise before publishing.** The E20 primary contrast is a real, large, within-run result that survived every attack I could mount against it; the report's interpretation layer (§0, §4.2, §4.3, §4.5, §7) does not survive, because it states conclusions its own §5 forbids and contradicts one of the two pre-registered readings it claims to quote verbatim.

## 1. Orientation

**The proposal in one sentence.** Two manipulations — the polarity of a `whenToUse` line and an entry's position in a 45-line picker — each moved the rate at which one model commits rather than asks by roughly 27–29 points, both in the direction opposite to prediction, and the author concludes that wording and order are each load-bearing, that the last position buys commitment at the price of wrong commits, and that batch-to-batch drift of about 12 points means only within-run contrasts may be claimed.

### Pre-mortem: it is November 2027 and this report failed

The report is published. §7's four rules are the only part anyone quotes, because they are short, imperative and each carries a number. Catalogue authors act on rule 4 ("expect the first entry to be penalised") and reorder forty-five modes per installation. Rule 2 is read as "do not add a requirement clause" and an exclusion clause is added to eleven `whenToUse` lines, three of which now exclude what the correct mode actually produces. Nobody re-reads §5, which says the interpretation is exploratory, because §5 is 8 bullets of negations and §7 is a table.

Eight months later a second brief is run and the exclusion form does not beat the requirement form — it is 6 points _behind_. The write-up cannot say the reversal was wrong, because the reversal was never registered as a hypothesis in the first place; it can only say "the first brief was different". The 12-point noise floor is cited in three later designs as a power input; none of them show that their control arm is stationary, and one of them ships with a 20-point within-run ramp that nobody looks for because the report's Instrument Proof table taught the field that a runs test on the outcome sequence is the guard for dependence. A runs test cannot see a trend — and the report made that the standard.

The reputational damage is not from the reversal. It is from the fact that the four imperatives were quotable and the eight caveats were not. **The failure mode is that summaries outlive caveats, and this report's summary is written as if the caveats had been resolved in its favour.**

Failure paths this pre-mortem adds that the report does not enumerate:

1. **The advice channel outlives the evidence channel.** §6 and §7 are structured for use; §5 is structured for absolution. Any reader who stops at §0 gets four rules and one number that no section of the body supports.
2. **The guard selection becomes a precedent.** The report reports one dependence guard (runs test) and omits the trend guard; §16.3 of the log already established that a guard must inspect the variable it protects. A trend in the control arm is invisible to both the verbatim guard and the runs test, and no guard in this programme would have found F-06.
3. **Interleaving is mistaken for randomisation.** The registration treats round-robin as the protection against ordering. It protects against a common trend. It cannot protect against an arm × time interaction, and the programme has now recorded one and not named it.

## 6. Weakest link and seams

**The single weakest link: the takeaway layer is not governed by the report's own evidential rules.** §5 is honest sentence by sentence; §0, §6, §7 and §8 are written as though §5 had not been. Every FATAL finding is an instance of the same seam.

Three structural seams, in order of how soon they will break:

1. **Registration ↔ report.** §4.3 quotes a reading as "verbatim", keeps the half that supports the reframing and reverses the half that constrains the advice (F-01). This seam breaks the moment a reader opens [`2026-11-17-e20c-preregistration.md`](2026-11-17-e20c-preregistration.md:71).
2. **E20c's mechanical column ↔ its unresolved mass.** One registered outcome (rival capture) is decided on a metric with 56 unresolved replies; the other two are point estimates whose plausible range spans the indecisive band (F-04, F-05, F-06). This seam breaks the moment the packet is adjudicated — and the packet exists, 241 entries, built and unopened.
3. **"Within-run" ↔ "stationary".** §4.6 uses "within one run" as a synonym for "valid". The E20c control arm shows the two are not the same thing (F-06, F-10). This seam breaks the first time anyone plots an arm against draw order.

## 7. Attacks I mounted that failed

Recorded because a review that only lists hits is not a review.

1. **"The 12.3-point drift is an unnoticed fixture change."** Failed. E20 arm B and E20c arm X use byte-identical prompts (`sha256 f77076ad904f…`, 6,160 bytes, 400 and 200 draws respectively). The drift is behavioural, not a fixture error.
2. **"The primary B−C contrast is an artefact of one batch."** Failed. Re-partitioned into four blocks of 100 draws per arm, the gap is −22, −29, −21, −35 — large, same-signed, never inside the 10–20 band. The primary result is stable in a way the E20c results are not.
3. **"The extraction numbers were massaged."** Failed. Every mechanical count reproduces to the unit with the repo's own parser, including the 21 bare slugs and the 2 wrong commits in E20c.
4. **"The bare-slug rule hid rival captures."** Failed. All 21 bare slugs name `explain`; not one names a rival. The rule is directionally sound even though its application is (F-05) uneven.
5. **"The blindness claim is a paper claim."** Failed. I checked all three packets directly: 246 / 132 / 241 entries, keys exactly `{id, brief, reply}`, no `ARM-` token, no run-id pattern, no ISO timestamp, no registered line text.
6. **"The p-values are wrong."** Failed. 7.13 × 10⁻²⁰, .014051, 1.31 × 10⁻⁵, 3.44 × 10⁻⁴, and a Newcombe interval of 21.8–33.1 against the reported 21.7–33.1.

## 8. Method rules M-1…M-9 — two tests

The rules ([`modes-experiment-log.md`](../research/modes-experiment-log.md:795)) read as genuine constraints, with one caveat: M-6 was adopted on 2026-11-17 _after_ E20 ran, so for E20 it is a description of the defect it was bought by, not a constraint the work obeyed. The log says so ("bought by **D-18**, E20"), which is honest.

**M-1 — "A headline automatic metric gets a manual pass before it is quoted."** **Not complied with, and this is the clearest rule violation in the report.** E20c's mechanical rates (47.0 / 60.0 / 71.5), its rival-capture count (1 vs 1) and its wrong-commit count (2) are quoted in §0.2, §3.5, §4.3, §6.1 and Annex H with **zero adjudication**. 241 of 600 replies have had no manual pass. The report knows the rule (it invokes the defect, D-19) and applies the rule everywhere except to the numbers it needs.

**M-9 — "Every contrast is within-run, or it is not a contrast."** **Not complied with.** §4.2's decomposition is a cross-batch subtraction (E20b P1 against E20c X against E20b P45), presented in §0 takeaway 2 without the exploratory label that §4.2 itself carries. Annex F is a cross-batch comparison used as evidence (legitimately — it is a variance statement, not a contrast — but the report does not distinguish the two uses). And §8's experiment 1 is designed to separate "first-entry penalty" from "rival at the end" precisely because the current answer requires a cross-batch comparison: the rule is being honoured by planning around it, not by obeying it.

**M-6** — complied with in the E20c registration (both signs, explicitly, including "any decrease → recorded as a reversal"), violated in the E20 registration, correctly recorded as a defect in Annex G. The report is honest here; the problem is that it treats the honesty as discharging the obligation (F-01 is the bill).

**M-3** — half complied with: the runs test was added, but not applied to E20c, which is the only arm where dependency is measurable (F-06).

## 11. Constructive counter-proposals

### CP-1 (recommended for F-01, F-02, F-03, F-18) — MITIGATE, text only, one day

1. Quote both registered E20c readings in full and mark the clause the report departs from as a departure, in Annex G, as Defect 5. Do not call a reversed reading "verbatim".
2. Move the first-entry decomposition, the "third lever" (composition) and the two-mechanism discussion into a clearly-labelled _Hypotheses for E22_ section. Keep §8's experiment 1 as the test.
3. Delete §7.2 (the skeleton) and §7.3 (the worked example). Retitle §7 "Hypotheses about line form, each tied to one unregistered contrast" and re-label all four rules with the evidence class they actually have: _reversed (E20), confounded (A vs B), unmeasured (E20c), exploratory (E20b)_.
4. Fix §4.5's "uncontrolled" → "one untested position".

**Trade-off:** the report loses its actionable surface, which is what makes it quotable. That is the point. **Residual risk if accepted as-is:** the four rules circulate without the three caveats, which is the pre-mortem above.

### CP-2 (mandatory for F-04, F-05, F-06) — REDESIGN the E20c section as bounds + stability, or hold it

Either (a) run the registered adjudication pass on the 241 and publish real counts, or (b) publish E20c as: _mechanical counts are lower bounds; `explain` Z−X lies between −28 and +52 points; rival capture is not estimable at this n; both registered readings are therefore undetermined._ Add the four-block stability table for X, Y and Z and the three runs tests. **Cost of (a):** one blind session, zero calls — the packet is built. **Cost of (b):** one paragraph. **What (b) costs the report:** takeaway 2's second half and §4.3's "the last slot is not a magnet" become "probably not a magnet, on 1 of 200 declarations with 28% of the arm unresolved".

### CP-3 (recommended for F-06, F-10, takeaway 4) — REDESIGN the reproducibility claim

Replace "12 points of batch noise" with what was measured: three same-condition observations of arm B/X at **47.5%, 59.3%, 47.0%**, a 90-prompt repeat agreement at chance, and a 24-point within-run ramp in the one arm that can be checked. Then state the rule that follows: _no contrast is valid unless its control arm is shown to be stationary over the run_, and add a four-block stability check to the standard report template — it is four lines of code and it would have caught F-06.

**Trade-off:** the report's advice gets narrower ("a within-run contrast whose control arm is flat"), but it becomes true. **Recommended option across all three: CP-1 + CP-2(b) + CP-3** — no calls, no new data, and the report can be published honestly before the next experiment.

## 12. Judgement on the specific questions asked

**"Did both primary effects come out opposite to prediction, and does that void E20's confirmatory status?"** E20's primary did: the ≥20-point reading asserts a direction and the observed direction is the mirror ([`2026-11-13-e20-preregistration.md`](2026-11-13-e20-preregistration.md:93)). Its confirmatory status for the _registered hypothesis_ is void — the hypothesis is refuted at _p_ < 10⁻¹⁹. E20b's primary did **not** come out opposite to its registered reading: that reading is sign-agnostic ("difference ≥ 20 points → position dominates… the routing conclusion is restated as being about list order") and it was met at 29 points. E20b failed its _premise_ (the literature's first-listed dominance) and satisfied its _threshold_. The report runs these two together and thereby gets to call both exploratory — which is convenient, because exploratory results are the ones you may interpret.

**"Is the sign-blindness handling honest, or post-hoc reinterpretation wearing a defect's clothes?"** Both, in different places, and the split is exact. The handling of _sign-blindness_ is honest: §3.2 says the registration has no reading for a reversal, Annex G records it as Defect 3, §5 says "not a confirmation". The handling of _E20c's reading_ is post-hoc reinterpretation wearing a defect's clothes: a frozen reading is quoted as applying "verbatim" while its operative clause is reversed (F-01). The honesty in one place is what makes the other look licensed.

**"Does the 12.3-point drift arithmetic hold, and what does it do to cross-experiment comparisons?"** It holds — 12.25 points, _z_ = 2.844, prompts byte-identical — and it invalidates §4.2, which compares an E20b arm with two E20c arms (F-02). It does **not** invalidate the report's own within-run contrasts, which §4.6 is right about in principle; but §4.6's chosen implementation of that principle ("within-run") is too weak, because the E20c control arm is non-stationary (F-06).

**"Is publishing E20c's figures defensible?"** As lower bounds, yes, and the report says so three times. As a "Corrected" column scoring 49.0 / 65.0 / 76.0 with 28–52% of each arm unresolved, no — the correction is unevenly applied (2.9% / 12.5% / 14.3% of the unresolved mass) so the three corrected rates are not on a common footing (F-05).

**"Could either extraction defect change a sign?"** D-19 cannot flip the sign of any `explain` contrast I can construct: applying the E20 arm-B yield (5.5%) to Z's unresolved mass and E20 arm-C's yield (21.4%) to X's moves Z−X from +24.5 to +14.8 — the _magnitude_ moves by ten points and out of the "largest effect" description, but not the sign. It **can** move the registered verdict on rival capture across the ≥20-point threshold, though only at a yield 3.4× anything observed. D-20 (gate rates) cannot change any sign, because the gate's only registered function is exclusion and clustering, and it passed both. What D-20 does do is hide the second-best piece of evidence for takeaway 4 (the gate's 47.5% for arm B).
