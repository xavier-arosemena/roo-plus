## 1. Executive summary

- **No published evidence supports, refutes, or bounds the 4,000/6,000-character instruction ceilings or the exact 100–200-character `whenToUse` band.** Every vendor that publishes a role-prompt ceiling publishes one _looser_ than 6,000 characters (Microsoft 8,000; Copilot 30,000; Codex 32 KiB; Windsurf 12,000 per file). Short-and-targeted wins directionally; the numbers are policy. **NONE** for the numbers, **MODERATE** for the direction. [S10][S8][S15][S13][S21][S22]
- **Persona prose is the weakest component of a role definition.** A 162-persona study across 2,410 factual questions finds no reliable accuracy gain and some harm; the same team's earlier improvement claim was withdrawn in a revised abstract. Role text works as scope, procedure and output contract. **MODERATE**. [S18][S19]
- **The routing-plane analogue is Claude Code's 15,000-token combined subagent-description budget.** Our Σ`whenToUse` ≤ 12,000 characters is roughly 5× tighter. **MODERATE** (vendor). [S5]
- **Description quality moves behaviour; verbosity costs turns.** Across 856 MCP tools, 97.1% carried at least one description smell and 56% failed to state purpose; augmentation raised success 5.85 pp but added 67% execution steps. **MODERATE**. [S25]
- **No per-item popularity metric exists for modes.** The marketplace is a bundled YAML with no count or ranking field; per-_extension_ counts do exist (Open VSX: Roo Code 2,038,264; Roo+ 18,691) but cannot be disaggregated per mode. **STRONG** (verified by execution). [R2][R3][S29][S30]
- **Install counts are contested where they exist.** A security vendor spoofed a skills-marketplace install metric; one directory removed counts as unverifiable. **MODERATE**/**WEAK**. [S47][S48]
- **Large collections are visible, not validated demand.** 150–200-agent collections hold 26k and 42k stars; stars measure attention, not per-role use. **STRONG** for counts, **NONE** for the inference. [S31]

**Reading of the numbers.** Ordered from tightest to loosest ceiling documented per role prompt: M365 8,000 chars → Windsurf 12,000 chars/file → Copilot agent 30,000 → Codex 32 KiB. (Windsurf's 6,000-character _global rules file_ — reported by secondary sources, not the vendor docs read here — is a whole-file cap, not a role prompt.) **Every documented role-prompt ceiling is at least 1.3× our proposed 6,000-character hard limit, and three of four are ≥ 5× it.** No vendor publishes a _lower_ ceiling than 6,000 characters for a role/system prompt. That is the single most useful budget fact in this workstream.

**Reading of the routing plane.** Only Anthropic publishes a budget for the _aggregate_ routing surface (15,000 tokens of subagent descriptions). Our 12,000-**character** preload routing budget is roughly 3k tokens — about one-fifth of that allowance. The 12k figure is therefore defensible as conservative policy; it is not derived from, or contradicted by, any published budget.

### A.2 Does persona/role prompting improve objective outcomes?

| Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Source                                                     | Grade        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |

| The same research line's earlier paper (role-play prompting, NAACL 2024) originally reported consistent improvement; the current abstract reports **no improvement over the no-persona control** across four model families                              | [S19] (arXiv 2308.07702 v2)                                | **MODERATE** |
| Independent replication-style test: 12 role prompts × 2,000 MMLU questions on GPT-4-turbo; results flat across personas, "genius" persona scored _worse_ than "idiot"                                                                                    | [S48] (secondary write-up of Learn Prompting's experiment) | **WEAK**     |
| Persona prompting reliably changes **style, tone, format** on open-ended tasks                                                                                                                                                                           | [S18] discussion; [S48]                                    | **MODERATE** |

**Implication for the document's §3.3 ("Modes are contracts, not personas").** The document's position is better supported than the opposite one. But note the internal tension flagged in §9(d): a 300–700-character `roleDefinition` budget is generous for something the evidence says is the least reliable component. The evidence supports _scope + standard + procedure_, not _character_.

### A.3 Instruction length, constraint count, and long-context recall

| Finding                                                                                                                                                                                              | Source                             | Grade                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Retrieval accuracy follows a U-shape: best when the relevant content is at the start or end, significantly worse in the middle                                                                       | [S20] (Liu et al., TACL 2024)      | **STRONG**                                                                |
| Across 18 frontier models, performance degrades as input length grows **even on simple tasks**; distractor sensitivity rises with length; degradation is continuous, not a cliff at the window limit | [S21] (Chroma, 2025-07-14)         | **MODERATE** (industry research, not peer-reviewed; heavily corroborated) |
| Instruction-following declines substantially as the **number of constraints** rises; GPT-4/GPT-3.5 reliably satisfy ~3 consecutive constraints, open models ~2                                       | [S22] (FollowBench, ACL 2024)      | **STRONG**                                                                |
| Composed (entangled) constraints expose further deficiencies beyond single-constraint benchmarks                                                                                                     | [S23] (ComplexBench, NeurIPS 2024) | **STRONG**                                                                |

**What this does and does not say about 4k/6k.** It says: (i) more instructions is not free; (ii) position matters, so the _end_ of a long doctrine block is a bad place for a critical rule; (iii) constraint _count_ matters at least as much as character count. It does **not** identify any character threshold, because none of these studies vary role-definition length in characters while holding task constant. **No verifiable data found** for a 4,000- or 6,000-character inflection point. The honest reading: 6,000 chars ≈ 1,500 tokens ≈ a few dozen constraints — already past the ~3-constraint reliable band in FollowBench, which is why the _contract template_ (few, high-value sections) probably matters more than the ceiling itself.

### A.4 Are negative instructions effective?

| Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Source                    | Grade                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Models perform **worse** on negated versions of prompts, and the gap does not shrink with scale (inverse scaling on negation across OPT/GPT-3 families; average performance near chance when positive/negative variants are averaged) | [S24] (arXiv 2209.12711)  | **MODERATE**–STRONG               |
| Microsoft's own best-practice guidance for declarative agents: "limit the instructions to what the agent **should** do, rather than what it should **not** do"                                                                        | [S11] [vendor]            | **MODERATE**                      |
| Practitioner counter-example: a negation-heavy prompt reportedly raised bug detection 39% → 89% on a code-_generation_ task, but the same approach underperformed on a code-_review_ task                                             | [S48] (single case study) | **WEAK**                          |
| "NegativePrompt" (IJCAI 2024) improves scores — but it injects negative **emotional stimuli**, not prohibitions; it must not be read as support for "do not…" clauses                                                                 | [S48]                     | **WEAK** (and frequently misread) |

**Assessment.** Negation _understanding_ is demonstrably weak, and vendor guidance says frame positively. The stronger form of the same intent is a **positive directive plus a named alternative**: "Only Edit `*.md`; route code changes to `code` mode" rather than "Do not edit code". The catalogue's "out-of-scope / where it belongs" section (§6 item 2 of the architecture doc) is exactly this pattern and is better supported than a list of prohibitions.

### A.6 Tool / agent selection accuracy vs description length and count

| Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Source                                     | Grade                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 856 tools across 103 MCP servers: **97.1%** of descriptions contain ≥1 quality "smell"; **56%** fail to state purpose clearly; augmenting descriptions raised task success by a median **5.85 pp** and partial goal completion by **15.12%**, but increased execution steps by **67.46%** | [S25] (arXiv 2602.14878, Feb 2026)         | **MODERATE**                                                                                                             |
| Retrieval-filtered tool selection raised selection accuracy **13.62% → 43.13%** and cut prompt tokens > 50% on the same benchmark                                                                                                                                                         | [S26] (RAG-MCP, arXiv 2505.03275)          | **MODERATE** (preprint)                                                                                                  |
| Tool _retrieval_ quality, not just model capability, degrades end-to-end task pass rate; conventional IR models underperform on tool retrieval                                                                                                                                            | [S27] (ToolRet, ACL 2025 Findings)         | **MODERATE**–STRONG                                                                                                      |
| Selection accuracy collapses as candidate tool count grows (commonly cited figures: noticeable degradation past ~15–20 tools; 43% → 2% from 4 → 51 tools on a BFCL scheduling task)                                                                                                       | [S29]-adjacent secondary blogs citing BFCL | **WEAK** (secondary; the direction is corroborated by [S26][S27], the exact numbers are not independently verified here) |
| "Namespacing tools to define clear boundaries" and prompting tool descriptions as carefully as prompts                                                                                                                                                                                    | [S4] [vendor]                              | **MODERATE**                                                                                                             |

**Mapping to modes.** A mode catalogue is a selection problem with the same structure as a tool catalogue: N candidates, one description each, chosen by a model. The transferable findings are (a) descriptions are the entire decision surface, (b) redundancy and ambiguity degrade selection, (c) filtering the candidate set is the highest-leverage fix, (d) richer descriptions buy accuracy at a turn-cost. **(d) is the reason a `whenToUse` budget must not be justified by "more description is better".** No study measures mode selection; the tool literature is an analogue, not a measurement. **NONE** for modes specifically.

### A.7 The specific question: is there evidence for 1–2 sentence `whenToUse` and 4k/6k ceilings?

**Direct answer: no.**

- **`whenToUse` 100–200 chars (hard 250).** No source proposes this band. The nearest published constraints are _description_ caps that are **5–10× larger** (agentskills 1,024 chars; M365 1,000 chars) and one aggregate budget (Claude Code 15,000 tokens). What the literature does support is _discriminability_: descriptions must let the selector distinguish candidates ([S5] "single out one subagent"; [S25] ambiguity and purpose-failure as the dominant smells). 100–200 chars is a defensible _design_ choice for a surface multiplied by installed-mode count; it is not an empirical optimum. **Grade: NONE for the number; MODERATE for the property it optimises.**
- **`customInstructions` ≤ 4,000 / hard 6,000.** No source. Six vendors publish ceilings, all ≥ 6,000 chars, none justifying the number. Direction (shorter, fewer constraints, critical rules not buried) is **MODERATE**. **Grade: NONE for the number.**
- **`roleDefinition` 300–700.** No source. **NONE.**
- **Preload Σ`whenToUse` ≤ 12,000 chars.** Analogue only (Claude Code's 15,000-token description budget), and ours is far tighter. **MODERATE as "conservative", NONE as "derived".**

### 4.3 Proxies that look like evidence but are not

1. **GitHub stars** — verified counts exist (42,172 / 26,321) but they measure the _collection's_ visibility. A 202-agent repo with 42k stars is evidence that assembling large catalogues is _legible_ to developers, not that any specific role is used.
2. **Marketplace "download" totals** — dominated by update traffic; VS Marketplace's own `updateCount` for Roo Code is 24.6M against 2.1M `install`.
3. **Search-volume proxies** — no source; excluded by rule.
4. **Marketing descriptions** ("Elite", "10×", "SOTA", "Master") — not findings. Notably, 202 of the 302 catalogue entries carry such language in `name`; that is a _supply_ artefact.
5. **Collection size as demand** ("we ship 302, therefore 302 are wanted") — circular.

### 4.4 The minimal in-product signal that respects no-telemetry

**Recommended: a local-only mode-usage ledger, user-visible, opt-in exportable.**

| Property                            | Design                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What is recorded                    | Per mode slug: count of activations, count of tasks completed in-mode, count of _immediate switches away_ (a proxy for misfire), last-used timestamp, install source (preload vs marketplace) |
| Where it lives                      | Local storage only (globalStorage JSON), never transmitted; displayed in Settings → Modes as a per-mode line ("used 12× in this workspace, last 2026-10-30")                                  |
| Export                              | Explicit user action writes a JSON/CSV the user can attach to an issue or paste publicly ("Export my mode usage")                                                                             |
| What it answers that proxies cannot | (a) which _modes_ are actually selected, (b) routing misfires (install → switch-away ratio), (c) preload waste (preloaded modes never used), (d) whether a mode earns its description cost    |
| What it still cannot answer         | Cross-user aggregates (correctly: that requires telemetry); intent ("wanted but absent")                                                                                                      |
| Cost                                | Small: a counter increment in the mode-switch path, a settings panel row, an export command. No network code.                                                                                 |

The second useful local signal is an **install log** for the bundled marketplace (which catalogue items a user installed, from which tab/filter) — again local-only, and only useful in aggregate if users export.

### 5.2 Products publishing concrete budgets (the most valuable data points)

Ranked by usefulness as an external anchor for our document:

1. **Claude Code — 15,000-token combined subagent-description budget.** The only published budget for the _routing_ plane, i.e. the exact cost surface our `whenToUse` budget models. Ours (12k chars ≈ 3k tokens) is 5× tighter.
2. **Microsoft 365 Copilot — 8,000-char `instructions` hard cap, 1,000-char `description`.** The only _schema-enforced_ role-prompt cap found. Sits just above our 6,000 hard ceiling.
3. **Windsurf — 12,000 chars/file, 6,000 chars global, 12,000 total with silent truncation.** The only product that _enforces a total budget by dropping content_, which is the same failure mode a preload cap prevents — and it validates the document's warning that silent truncation is worse than a build-time gate.
4. **OpenAI Codex — 32 KiB `project_doc_max_bytes`.** A hard, configurable byte cap on always-on instructions.
5. **GitHub Copilot — 30,000-char agent prompt.** A loose ceiling that effectively says "we do not expect you to hit this".
6. **Cursor — 500 lines.** Line-based, not character-based; equivalent to roughly 20–30k characters of prose.
7. **agentskills.io — 1,024-char description, 500-line body.** The only _specification-level_ constraint on a description, and directly analogous to `whenToUse`/skill descriptions.

**No product publishes a per-mode/per-agent character budget for the doctrine plane below 6,000 characters.** That absence is itself the answer to the task's specific question.

### 5.3 Structural observations worth carrying into the document

- **Nobody else ships 302 roles.** The largest first-party surfaces are 5 built-ins (Roo/Cline/Kilo) plus user-authored modes. Large numbers appear only in _third-party collections_ (§6.4), not in products.
- **The industry is converging on two planes, not three**: an always-on instruction file (AGENTS.md / rules / CLAUDE.md) and a lazily-loaded skill (SKILL.md). Roo's _mode_ is the outlier in being a third, selector-driven plane with its own routing text. Claude Code's subagent `description` budget shows that even the mature version of this pattern needs an explicit aggregate cap.
- **Capability is expressed as a permission map more often than as tool groups** in the newer generation (Kilo `permission:`, Copilot `tools`, Claude `tools`, Codex sandbox). Our `groups` + `fileRegex` is a coarser version of the same idea; the trend supports the document's Phase 4 direction (least privilege, explicit MCP list).
- **Discovery is filesystem-first everywhere except marketplaces.** No competitor publishes per-role ranking; the two that aggregate at scale (skill registries) have had their install-count metrics attacked as invalid.

## 7. Workstream E — Additional material judged relevant

### 7.1 A2A Agent Cards: description-as-routing, without a selector

- In A2A v1.0, `skills` is a **required** array on the Agent Card; each `AgentSkill` has `id`, `name`, `description`, `tags`, plus optional `examples`, `inputModes`, `outputModes`. The specification describes a skill as "largely a descriptive concept" [S46]. Grade **MODERATE** (spec-derived documentation).
- **No A2A operation selects a skill** — `SendMessage` carries no skill field; the receiving agent decides how to handle the message. Skills exist for the _reader_ of the card [S46].
- **Implication:** the standards space independently converged on "name + description + tags, read by a chooser", which is exactly our routing plane — but it declined to create a selector protocol. That is mild support for keeping `whenToUse` short and tag-like rather than procedural.
- **No published cap** on number of skills or description length was found. **NONE.**

### 7.2 Skills registries: the only place per-item counts exist, and why they are weak

- `skills.sh` publishes an install-count leaderboard for skills (secondary descriptions) [S48].
- One directory (Skills Directory) **removed install counts in May 2026**, stating there was no credible answer to "how do you know?", and labels its quality ranking as editorial [S48]. Grade **WEAK** (self-report by a competitor directory).
- Orca Security demonstrated **install-count inflation via unauthenticated GET requests** on a skills marketplace, plus non-deterministic scanning and silent skill override [S47]. Grade **MODERATE** (single-origin vendor research, specific and mechanistically plausible).
- **Implication for Roo+:** if a popularity metric were ever displayed, it would be an attack surface and a trust liability. Editorial curation + local usage is the defensible alternative.

### 7.3 Eval-driven demand (my own addition — labelled as analysis, not a finding)

The question "which roles are needed" can be reframed as **coverage of failure modes** rather than popularity: a role is justified when a named, reproducible failure occurs in a golden task that no existing role catches. This is consistent with the tool-selection literature (filtering the candidate set beats growing it, [S26]) and with the document's Phase 1 priority. **No published methodology for deriving roles this way was found** — this is a proposal, not evidence. It has one property the proxies lack: it produces a _falsifiable_ justification per mode ("without this mode, eval E fails").

### 7.4 Jobs-to-be-done from issue trackers (my own addition — method sketch)

Roo Code was archived with **1,031 open issues** (GitHub API, 2026-11-04). A role-demand study could classify issues by _missing capability_ (e.g. "review my Terraform", "write migration") rather than by product, producing a task-type distribution. Not attempted here: issue bodies were not read, and unlabelled issue titles are a weak classifier. **NONE** until done.

### (c) A catalogue / preload policy

- **Keep the catalogue large; keep the preload small and frozen.** The tool-selection evidence says filtering the candidate set is the highest-leverage intervention ([S26] 3.2× accuracy, >50% token cut), and the preload _is_ that filter.
- **Add "one mode per failure mode / job", not per persona.** Supported by persona evidence ([S18]) and by redundancy-degrades-selection ([S25]).
- **Do not add a popularity ranking, and do not display marketplace install counts as mode popularity.** No per-mode metric exists (STRONG, V1/V4), and where counts exist they are contested (MODERATE, [S47][S48]).
- **Do not use collection size as a quality claim.** "302 modes" is a supply fact; the only published demand side is attention to collections (stars), which does not disaggregate.

### (d) Contradictions with the current document (flagged, not resolved)

1. **`roleDefinition` investment vs persona evidence.** §3.3 says modes are contracts, §4 budgets 300–700 chars of role text. These can coexist, but the document should say what the 300–700 chars are _for_ (scope, standard, refusal, handoff) — otherwise the budget invites exactly the persona prose the evidence disfavours. **Flagged in §10 C7.**
2. **"not-for" inside the most expensive field.** §4 requires `whenToUse` to contain trigger, domain noun **and** _not-for_, within 100–200 chars, on a surface multiplied by installed modes. No external source requires _not-for_; it is a design choice that competes for the same characters as the trigger. **Unresolved tension between §3.1 and §4** — consider moving _not-for_ to `description` (picker-only, zero routing cost) or to the doctrine's out-of-scope section.
3. **Budget framing.** §4 presents budgets as "derived from the measured baseline and the cost model". That is true for Σ`whenToUse`, but not for the per-field ceilings, which are policy. Recommended wording: "cost-derived" for the aggregate, "policy, benchmarked against published vendor ceilings" for the per-field numbers. **Flagged in §10 C6.**
4. **"Instructions at that scale displace the task they are meant to guide" (§2.1).** Directionally supported ([S20][S21][S22]) but stated as a measurement. No study measures a 6,000-char role prompt. Reword to "consistent with long-context and constraint-count findings" and keep the measured inventory as the actual evidence.
5. **Phase 1.2 "golden task per preloaded mode"** implies 90 × 3 evals before budgets tighten. The literature supports evals as the arbiter ([S16]), but there is no external evidence that 90 modes can be maintained behaviourally. **Flag:** the Phase 1 scope may need a sampled subset (e.g. the 20 modes carrying most of the preload's routing cost) before it is complete.
