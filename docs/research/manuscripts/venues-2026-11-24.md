**Checked:** 2026-11-24 UTC. Fee status read from publisher pages and APC price files; DOI presence and DOAJ listing machine-checked against the Crossref and DOAJ APIs on the same date. Two widely repeated secondary claims were falsified by those checks and are flagged below.

**Status of the manuscript:** formatted for Academia.edu Journals _AI and Applications_. **Do not continue venue-specific formatting until a venue is chosen.** The structure, the tables, the figures and the statements survive most conversions with hours of work, not days; the abstract shape and the citation style do not.

## 1. The trigger

_AI and Applications_ is a tiered-APC journal:

| Article type | Type A              | Type B      | Type C      | Type D       |
| ------------ | ------------------- | ----------- | ----------- | ------------ |
| Charge       | **$2,000 / €1,900** | $900 / €850 | $400 / €375 | Fully waived |

A Research Article falls in Type A. That is out of scope for an unfunded solo author, and no discretionary waiver applies: Springer Nature, Elsevier, ACM and IEEE all decline hybrid-journal APC waivers on financial-need grounds, because the subscription route is the free option. Waivers that do exist are country-based (Research4Life, World Bank income classification), and a Dutch small company does not qualify.

## 2. What "free" has to mean here

Three distinct things get confused, and only two of them are actually free:

1. **Diamond open access** — no author fee, no reader fee. Rare and valuable: JAIR, TMLR, JMLR, JOSS.
2. **Subscription/hybrid route** — the publisher charges nothing if you decline open access. The paper is paywalled but citable, and green open access (the accepted manuscript) is usually permitted after an embargo. Most established journals work this way.
3. **APC waiver** — an exception granted by the publisher. For this author, effectively none.

## 6. Free routes that do not work, and why

## 8. Caution list

1. **ACM's subscription route ended 2026-01-01.** TOSEM costs $1,450 in 2026 and $1,800 from 2027; membership halves rather than removes it. TOCHI is in the same position.
2. **IEEE TSE overlength charges are mandatory**, $220 per printed page beyond twelve double-column pages. With eight tables and three figures this manuscript is at real risk of crossing that line, which converts a free venue into a paid one.
3. **Hybrid "free" means paywalled, not waived.** EMSE and ASE at €2,790, Machine Learning at €2,790, AIJ at $4,260. Reader access is lost unless the accepted manuscript is self-archived.
4. **TMLR and JMLR have no DOIs** — verified mechanically, not inferred. Any plan that assumes a DOI will arrive at TMLR is wrong.
5. **Two references were wrong and are now corrected**: Katz & Byrne is 198–220, not 243–67, and the first author is Katz MA, not Katz SM. They were the two entries nobody had re-opened.
6. **Double-anonymous venues.** EMSE and TMLR anonymise; the preprint may exist but must not be linked from the submission.
7. **The burned fixture is not a problem for any shortlisted venue.** None mandates depositing the test fixture; JAIR wants a reproducibility checklist, Elsevier titles want a data-availability statement, EMSE's open-science badges are voluntary.

## 9. Recommendation

**Post the preprint first, then submit to one of two venues.** The order matters because the preprint costs nothing, establishes priority, and gives the work a citable handle while review takes months.

1. **arXiv (cs.SE, cross-listed cs.HC), this week.** Free, immediate, citable, and permitted by every venue shortlisted here.
2. **Empirical Software Engineering — first choice.** Best fit in the list for what this paper actually is: an empirical, pre-registered study of an agent's behaviour under a configuration change, with the instrument reported as part of the result. Zero cost on the subscription route, DOI, Scopus and SCIE, and the lowest reformatting cost of any journal here (~2–3 hours). The one adaptation is re-heading the abstract to Context / Objective / Method / Results / Conclusions and trimming it to 250 words.
3. **Information and Software Technology — fast, near-zero-reformat fallback.** Elsevier numbered references are the closest house style to the current manuscript, there is no publication fee, and the reported first-decision median is seven days.
4. **JAIR — choose it if free-to-read matters more than fit.** Diamond open access, a real DOI, Scopus and SCIE, and the cheapest "prestige per euro" available. The costs are an author–year conversion, a mandatory reproducibility checklist, and a scope argument a reviewer may not accept: JAIR publishes AI research, and this is a measurement study of an interface.

**Do not choose** ACM TOSEM or TOCHI (mandatory APC from 2026), IEEE TSE (overlength exposure), JMLR or TMLR (no DOI), JOSS (out of scope), or any hybrid's paid open-access option.

## 12. Two clarifications this search made necessary

**"Post the preprint" means arXiv, and it is not a journal.** A preprint server hosts the manuscript publicly before peer review. It is free, it assigns a DOI of the form `10.48550/arXiv.XXXX.XXXXX`, it timestamps priority, and essentially every journal shortlisted here permits it. It is not indexed as a journal and it does not involve review. Practical notes: pick `cs.SE` as the primary category with `cs.HC` cross-listed; choose the CC BY 4.0 licence rather than arXiv's default non-exclusive licence, so the preprint can be reused and quoted; a first submission to a category by an author without an academic affiliation may require an endorsement code from an established submitter, which the submission form will state; and moderation can take a few days. Posting it does not consume the journal submission, and it gives the paper a citable handle while review takes months.

**"Zero cost on the subscription route" does not mean you subscribe to anything.** It describes who pays for reading, not who pays for publishing. In a subscription or hybrid journal the publisher's revenue comes from libraries and institutions that license the journal; the author submits, is reviewed, and is published at no charge, and the published article sits behind that licence. There is no subscription to buy, no minimum term, and at EMSE no submission fee, no page charge and no colour charge on that route. The alternative is optional: you _may_ pay an APC (€2,790 at EMSE; £2,390 / $3,390 in other currencies) to make your own article open access, and declining it is the default free path. What you give up is reader-side access, which is largely recovered by depositing the accepted manuscript in a repository after the embargo — green open access — and by the arXiv preprint that step 1 already produces.
