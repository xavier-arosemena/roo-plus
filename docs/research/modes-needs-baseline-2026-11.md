## 1. Executive answer

**The recommended axis set is twelve dimensions of software work, drawn from occupational, standards and empirical sources — not from the repository's ten house categories:**

1. Requirements & problem framing
2. Architecture & design
3. Implementation & construction
4. Comprehension & navigation
5. Debugging & defect localisation
6. Testing & verification
7. Refactoring & evolution
8. Review & quality assurance
9. Security & privacy
10. Delivery, build & operations
11. Data, schema & persistence
12. Documentation, knowledge & handover

**Their provenance differs by axis, and that is a feature, not a compromise.** The lifecycle-shaped axes (1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 10, 12) are named by ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207 processes [S4] and SWEBOK knowledge areas [S3]; the occupational axes (1, 3, 5, 6, 8, 10, 11) are named by O*NET task statements and detailed work activities [S1]; the *friction\* axes (4, 5, 7) are named by developer-question studies [S10] and by the only public taxonomy of questions asked of a repository-aware assistant [S11]. No single source produces all twelve. That is the finding: **no existing taxonomy covers both the work and the asking.**

**The inventory is 119 jobs** — a task with a trigger and a definition of done — across the twelve axes, at 8–12 jobs per axis. 18 are ADOPTED from a single named taxonomy element, 83 are CLUSTERED from two or more named elements, and 18 are DERIVED (15.1%, under the 20% ceiling) and listed separately in §5.5 so a reviewer can attack them individually.

**Three measured facts about the current catalogue follow from putting it on these axes** (method in §7; supply mapping is mine, single-analyst, from [`AGENT_CATALOG.md`](../../custom-modes/AGENT_CATALOG.md:1)):

- **Comprehension has no mode and 49% of the questions.** 182 of 371 hard-to-answer questions in the largest developer-question study fall in the comprehension axis (rationale, intent, history, location, contracts, control/data flow, types) [S10]; "explaining or understanding existing code" is the _second most adopted_ AI task at 78% [S13]. No catalogue mode is owned by a comprehension job — **0 of 302, 0 of the 90 preloaded** (§7.1).
- **Debugging has two modes, none preloaded, and is the most common agent-facing task class.** 62.7% of 500 SWE-bench questions classify as code location / bug identification [S11]; debugging is 65% adopted [S13] and 26/371 of hard questions [S10]. The catalogue's DBG share is 0.7% of slugs and 0.0% of preload slots. (The built-in `debug` mode sits outside the 302 by construction, so the true preload gain when ADR-6 folds built-ins in is one mode, not zero.)
- **40% of the catalogue has no coding job.** 69 slugs are non-software professions (legal, marketing, PM, research, SEO), 20 are non-software creative/product artefacts (style pastiches, brand, motion), and 32 are agent-process modes (swarms, orchestrators, personas) that describe _how the agent works_, not _what a developer needs done_. Together they hold **25 of 90 preload slots and 27.9% of the preload's routing characters** — while the two axes with the strongest external demand signal hold 0 slots.

**The single biggest risk** is not a wrong axis — the axes triangulate well — but **category error in the counting unit**. This inventory measures jobs. The catalogue contains specialisations (43 of 78 IMP slugs are language/framework/platform-specific), duplicates, and non-coding roles. If ADR-6 reads "119 jobs" as "119 modes and the rest is waste", it will delete legitimate specialisation coverage; if it reads "302 modes" as "302 jobs", it keeps a ratio of 1.5 slugs per job in the engineering axes and 0 in the two highest-demand ones. The instrument in §7 exists to prevent both.

## 2. Method & limitations

### 2.1 What was done

Five instruments were assembled, each chosen because it fails differently from the others:

| Instrument                                                                                                                                                                               | What it measures                                         | Why it is in the set                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Occupational task statements** (O\*NET 30.1, four software occupations, 88 rated tasks) [S1]                                                                                           | What jobs are _accountable for_, with importance ratings | Gives a quantitative, externally-owned denominator; ages slowly              |
| **Lifecycle process standards** (ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207, 30 processes; SWEBOK v4.0a, 18 knowledge areas) [S4][S3]                                                                            | What the _discipline_ says the work consists of          | Gives stable, separable names and boundaries; licence-encumbered but citable |
| **Role/skill frameworks** (ESCO software developer, 23 essential + 63 optional skills; SFIA 9 levels) [S7][S8]                                                                           | What competences are _recognised_                        | Tests whether an axis is a real competence or a house invention              |
| **Developer-question studies** (LaToza & Myers 2010: 179 developers, 371 questions, 21 categories; Ojha et al. 2026: 86-node taxonomy of questions to a repo-aware assistant) [S10][S11] | What developers are _stuck on_                           | The only instrument that measures friction rather than assignment            |
| **Task-level AI-use survey** (Sonar 2026: n=1,149, 11 named tasks, adoption + effectiveness) [S13]                                                                                       | What developers _already delegate_                       | Closest available proxy for agent demand, with a commercial-bias caveat      |

Two supporting instruments are used narrowly: DORA's capability catalogue for delivery/operations [S14], and the DevEx triad (feedback loops, cognitive load, flow) [S15] only as vocabulary for what makes a trigger _a trigger_.

### 2.2 The ordering rule that mattered

The catalogue was **not** used to derive axes. It was used exactly twice: (a) to sort 302 existing slugs _into_ externally-defined axes, so that supply could be compared to need; (b) to test whether an axis is empty in our own supply. Axis names, boundaries and definitions come from the instruments above. The house ten-category taxonomy in [`AGENT_CATALOG.md`](../../custom-modes/AGENT_CATALOG.md:1) is treated as metadata for sorting, never as evidence. Where the two disagree, the external source wins and the disagreement is recorded (§4.5).

### 2.3 Grading

| Grade        | Meaning                                                                                           | Sources in this report                                                                             |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **STRONG**   | Primary standard, official statistical/occupational database, or peer-reviewed study read in full | O\*NET DB, SWEBOK, ISO 12207, ISO 25010, ISTQB, ESCO, SFIA, NIST SSDF, LaToza & Myers, Ojha et al. |
| **MODERATE** | Industry research, vendor survey, preprint, or a peer-reviewed source accessed secondhand         | DORA, Sonar, Beyer et al. (secondhand), DevEx article                                              |

### 2.4 Limitations a reviewer should hold against this report

1. **Triggers are reconstructed, not observed.** No per-mode or per-task telemetry exists in this product, and the prior research established that no per-item demand metric exists on any marketplace surface [S16 §4]. Every trigger sentence is an inference from a source element plus the agent-doability filter. The _jobs_ are sourced; the _phrasing of the situation_ is mine.
2. **O\*NET measures assignment, not demand.** It contains 88 rated tasks across four software occupations but **no task statement for "understand the code"** — comprehension is implicit in every task and therefore invisible to it. That is why a comprehension axis can be 0% of occupational task mass and 49% of hard questions. Neither number is wrong; they measure different things (§4.2).
3. **Security is under-sampled, not unimportant.** O*NET security tasks live mostly in occupation 15-1212 (Information Security Engineers), which was not pulled. Security's N is therefore thin on *request* evidence and strong on *obligation\* evidence (SSDF, SWEBOK KA13).
4. **The Sonar survey is vendor research** (Sonar sells code-quality tooling) and samples developers who already use AI. Its adoption percentages are direction-of-travel, not population estimates.
5. **The 2026 EMNLP taxonomy's full tree is an image.** Ten top-level intents and the SWE-bench profile are readable in text; the 86 nodes are not machine-extractable (§9).
6. **One analyst, one pass.** The catalogue→axis mapping (302 slugs) and the family grouping in §8 are judgement calls. They are stated as rules so they can be re-run, not as measurements.
7. **No usage data means N cannot be weighted by frequency.** Stated as a rule, not a caveat: we do not weight by usage because we have none.
8. **One of the four O\*NET occupations is rated on 2017 data.** Occupation 15-1253 (QA Analysts and Testers) was last rated in 2017 — before CI-era practice, let alone agents — and its 27 tasks are 30% of the rated pool. That is the main reason testing leads N₁ (22.1%). The TST band survives on independent evidence (75% delegation [S13], ISTQB and SWEBOK as specifications), but its N₁ share should be read as an upper bound, not a point estimate.

### 3.1 What the evaluation establishes

1. **No single taxonomy is adoptable.** The two most complete (12207, SWEBOK) are lifecycle-shaped and have no friction dimension; the two friction-shaped (LaToza, Ojha) have no lifecycle and no security. The axis set must be assembled, and each axis must name its own source.
2. **Granularity is where taxonomies actually differ.** ISTQB's seven activities and O\*NET's 88 tasks are both "testing"; one is a step list, one is a deliverable list. The job unit (§6) is the only unit that reconciles them.
3. **Occupational taxonomies age slowly, trend reports age fast.** O*NET ratings span 2017 (QA) to 2025 (software developers) and remain usable for task *names\* long after the weights drift; DORA/Sonar percentages will be wrong within two years and are used only as cross-checks, never as denominators.
4. **Licensing is a real constraint.** ISO 12207 and ISO 25010 are citable but not reproducible; the report quotes process and characteristic _names_ only, which is what an axis needs.

## 4. The adopted axes

Each axis states: definition, the artefact that proves it done, primary source, and why it is separable from its nearest neighbour.

### 4.1 A1 — Requirements & problem framing

**Definition.** Turning a stated want into something buildable and checkable: eliciting intent, resolving conflict, specifying acceptance, deciding feasibility. **Done-artefact:** a decision-ready specification (criteria, assumptions, open questions). **Source:** SWEBOK KA1 (elicitation, analysis, specification, validation) [S3]; ISO 12207 6.4.1–6.4.3 (business/mission analysis → stakeholder needs → system/software requirements) [S4]; O*NET 15-1252 task 21662 (analyse user needs, feasibility within time and cost) [S1]. **Separable from ARC** because the artefact is a statement of *what/why*, not a structure of *how\*; from DOC because the output constrains the build rather than describing it afterwards. **9 jobs.**

### 4.2 A2 — Architecture & design

**Definition.** Choosing structure: boundaries, interfaces, data models, quality-attribute tactics, and the trade-offs recorded. **Done-artefact:** a decision with alternatives and consequences (ADR, contract, design note). **Source:** SWEBOK KA2–KA3 [S3]; ISO 12207 6.4.4–6.4.5 (architecture definition, design definition) [S4]; O*NET 21664/21667/21672 [S1]. **Separable from IMP** because the artefact is a commitment made *before* code and persists after it; from REQ because it answers *how\*, and its failure mode is a cost that appears later. **10 jobs.**

### 4.3 A3 — Implementation & construction

**Definition.** Producing code that satisfies a specification, including the integration of libraries and the handling of errors. **Done-artefact:** working code plus tests that fail before and pass after. **Source:** SWEBOK KA4 [S3]; ISO 12207 6.4.7 (implementation) [S4]; O*NET 21667 and 15-1251 task 1273 [S1]; Sonar's largest adoption block (90%) [S13]. **Separable from REF** because behaviour *changes\*; from ARC because the decision is already made; from TST because the tests are the evidence, not the deliverable. **12 jobs** — the axis with the most specialisation pressure (43 of 78 catalogue slugs here are stack-specific, §7.2).

### 4.4 A4 — Comprehension & navigation

**Definition.** Answering questions about existing code: what it does, where it is, why it is that way, what depends on it, what would break. **Done-artefact:** an _answer_ with evidence — a map, a path, a rationale — not a change. **Source:** LaToza & Myers 2010 — rationale (42 reports), intent & implementation (32), history (23), location (13), control flow (19), contracts (17), data flow (14), type relationships (15), dependencies (5), method properties (2), architecture (11) [S10]; Ojha et al. 2026 top-level intents _Repository Understanding and Summary_, _Code Understanding and Explanation_, _Code Reasoning and Decision Rationale_ [S11]; Sonar "explaining existing code" 78% adoption, 66% effectiveness [S13]. **Separable from DBG** because no failure is required to start it and no fix is required to finish it; from REF because nothing changes; from DOC because the audience is the developer, not a reader of a document. **10 jobs — and currently 0 modes.** This is the axis O\*NET cannot see (§2.4) and the one the evidence ranks highest (§7.4).

### 4.5 A5 — Debugging & defect localisation

**Definition.** From an observed wrong behaviour to a named cause, with a reproduction and a decision (fix, workaround, escalate, or hand off). **Done-artefact:** a causal claim with evidence plus a next action. **Source:** LaToza _Debugging_ (26 reports) [S10]; O\*NET 21670 ("modify existing software to correct errors") and 15-1251 tasks 1267/1278/1275 [S1]; ISTQB execution ("anomalies are analysed to identify likely causes") [S6]; Ojha et al.'s SWE-bench profile — 62.7% code location/bug identification [S11]; Sonar 65% adoption, 44% effectiveness [S13]. **Separable from UND** (fix required, not just an answer) and from TST (the test is the symptom, not the deliverable). **11 jobs.**

### 4.6 A6 — Testing & verification

**Definition.** Producing evidence that behaviour matches expectation: designing checks, implementing them, running them, and judging the result. **Done-artefact:** executable or recorded evidence mapped to a criterion. **Source:** ISTQB's seven activity groups [S6]; SWEBOK KA5 (levels, techniques, measures, process) [S3]; ISO 12207 6.4.9 verification [S4]; O*NET 15-1253 — 16 of 88 rated tasks [S1]; Sonar 75% adoption / 59% effectiveness [S13]. **Separable from REV** because evidence is *executed* rather than *read\*; from DBG because the question is "does it meet the criterion", not "why did it fail". **11 jobs.**

### 4.7 A7 — Refactoring & evolution

**Definition.** Changing structure while preserving behaviour: simplifying, de-duplicating, upgrading, modernising, deprecating. **Done-artefact:** same behaviour, better structure, proven by unchanged tests. **Source:** SWEBOK KA7 (maintenance techniques, key issues) [S3]; ISO 12207 6.4.13 maintenance [S4]; LaToza _Refactoring_ (25) and _Implications_ (21) [S10]; DORA code maintainability and dependency currency [S14]; Sonar 72% adoption / 43% effectiveness — the widest adoption-effectiveness gap in the survey [S13]. **Separable from IMP** by the behaviour-preservation contract; from REV because the change is made, not judged. **9 jobs.**

### 4.8 A8 — Review & quality assurance

**Definition.** Judging someone else's change or artefact against standards, and issuing a verdict. **Done-artefact:** findings with severity and a verdict (approve, block, waive). **Source:** SWEBOK KA12 (quality fundamentals, management, assurance) [S3]; ISO 12207 6.3.8 quality assurance [S4]; O*NET 15-1254 task 14700 ("evaluate code … meets industry standards") and 15-1253 tasks 14649/14653/14658 [S1]; the *Review\* question class in Stack Overflow taxonomies [S12, secondhand]; Sonar 55% adoption / 47% effectiveness [S13]. **Separable from TST** (reading vs executing) and from REF (judgement vs change). **9 jobs.**

### 4.9 A9 — Security & privacy

**Definition.** Reducing the probability and impact of adversarial or privacy failure: hardening inputs, proving boundaries, responding to vulnerabilities, proving provenance. **Done-artefact:** a control plus evidence it holds (test, attestation, SBOM, threat model). **Source:** SWEBOK KA13 (new in v4) [S3]; NIST SSDF PO/PS/PW/RV [S9]; O*NET 15-1254 task 14723 and 15-1253 task 14658 [S1]; Ojha et al.'s *Code Quality and Security Review* intent [S11]. **Separable from REV** because the adversary is explicit and the evidence is adversarial testing, not style. **10 jobs.** *N caveat:\* thin on request evidence, strong on obligation evidence (§2.4).

### 4.10 A10 — Delivery, build & operations

**Definition.** Getting a change safely into production and keeping it there: pipelines, releases, incidents, observability, infrastructure, cost. **Done-artefact:** a service state (green, deployed, recovered) with a timeline. **Source:** SWEBOK KA6 (Software Engineering Operations — new in v4) [S3]; ISO 12207 6.4.10–6.4.12 (transition, operation, maintenance interface) [S4]; O\*NET 21671/21666 and 15-1253 tasks 14654/14656 [S1]; DORA CI/CD, deployment automation, flexible infrastructure [S14]. **Separable from IMP** because success is measured on the running system, not on the diff. **11 jobs.**

### 4.11 A11 — Data, schema & persistence

**Definition.** Changing and querying stored state safely: schema evolution, query performance, backfills, retention, format conversion. **Done-artefact:** data correctness proven by reconciliation or contract. **Source:** O*NET 21676 ("store, retrieve, and manipulate data") and 15-1254 task 14704 ("develop databases"), plus the DWA "assess database performance" [S1]; DORA *database change management\* [S14]; SWEBOK KA3 data design [S3]. **Separable from IMP** because the failure mode is irreversible and the evidence is reconcilable counts, not a green build. **8 jobs** — the axis with the highest DERIVED share (4/8), which is disclosed.

### 4.12 A12 — Documentation, knowledge & handover

**Definition.** Making work legible: change descriptions, API references, decisions, setup, status, history. **Done-artefact:** a text a specific reader can act on. **Source:** SWEBOK KA9 (communication) and KA8 (SCM history) [S3]; ISO 12207 6.3.6 information management and 6.3.3 decision management [S4]; O*NET 21673 ("prepare reports or correspondence") and 15-1254 task 14722 [S1]; LaToza *Teammates* (16) and *History\* (23) [S10]; DORA documentation quality [S14]; Sonar documentation 74% adoption, 74% effectiveness (highest effectiveness) [S13]. **Separable from UND** because the output is durable and written for someone else. **9 jobs.**

### 4.13 What the axes deliberately do not include

- **Domains, languages, frameworks, platforms** (React, Rust, AWS, embedded). These are _specialisation modifiers_ of an axis, not axes. Evidence: 43 of 78 implementation slugs are stack-specific while the implementation axis has 12 jobs — a 3.6:1 specialisation ratio inside one axis (§7.2). Treating stacks as axes is precisely how a 302-mode catalogue arises from 119 jobs.
- **Quality attributes** (performance, accessibility, i18n, safety, compatibility). These are _constraints_ attached to jobs, sourced from ISO/IEC 25010:2023 [S5]. A job "implement a feature" may carry the constraint "p95 < 200 ms"; the constraint is not a separate job unless the trigger is a failed target (I10, T11, D2).
- **Roles and professions** (legal, marketing, project management, hiring). They are work, and the catalogue contains 69 slugs for them — but they are not _coding_ work, and an agent mode for them is not a mode in this system's sense (no code capability required). They are listed in §8.4, not judged.
- **Agent process** (swarms, orchestrators, personas, task distributors). These describe _how_ an agent is composed, and their natural home is the runtime or a skill, not a job owner. They are listed in §8.4 as META.

## 5. The job inventory

### 5.1 Unit and labels

A **job** is one recurring situation brought to an agent, with a trigger and a definition of done that a single owner could be accountable for.

- **ADOPTED** — the job is a named element of one source, lightly reworded (18).
- **CLUSTERED** — two or more named elements merged; the elements are listed in the source column (83).
- **DERIVED** — no source element; the inference is stated, and the job appears again in §5.5 for challenge (18, 15.1%).

The definition-of-done column is always authored: it is the operationalisation, not the source. Source abbreviations: `ON-SD` O\*NET 15-1252.00, `ON-PR` 15-1251.00, `ON-QA` 15-1253.00, `ON-WD` 15-1254.00 [S1]; `SWEBOK KAn` [S3]; `ISO 12207 n.n.n` [S4]; `ISTQB` [S6]; `LT <category> (n reports)` LaToza & Myers 2010 [S10]; `EMNLP <intent>` Ojha et al. 2026 [S11]; `SONAR <task> n%` [S13]; `ESCO <skill>` [S7]; `SSDF <practice>` [S9]; `DORA <capability>` [S14]; `ISO 25010` [S5].

## 6. Clustering method

### 6.1 The rule

1. **Pool.** Every source element is entered as `(source, identifier, text)`: task statements, ISTQB activities, SWEBOK topics, question categories, survey use cases. 88 O\*NET tasks + 30 12207 processes (names only) + ~40 SWEBOK topics + 21 LaToza categories + 10 EMNLP intents + 11 survey tasks.
2. **Normalise.** Rewrite each element as _actor · action · artefact · outcome_, stripping organisational and occupational modifiers ("supervise", "train users", "confer with managers", "visit beta sites").
3. **Filter.** Drop elements that are organisational (hire, budget, procure, staff), that require authority an agent does not have, or that are vendor-specific tool operations. Dropped weight was 13.2% of the O\*NET mass — reported, not hidden.
4. **Merge** when two elements share **the same trigger class** and **the same done-artefact kind**. Same trigger + same evidence = one job.
5. **Split** when any of these differs:
    - the done-artefact _kind_ (answer vs change vs verdict vs state);
    - the first action the trigger demands (reproduce vs read vs run);
    - the capability grant required (read-only vs edit vs execute) — a split that matters to this system specifically, because a mode's grants are enforced;
    - the audience of the output (self/team/reviewer/auditor).
6. **Do not split** by language, framework, domain, or tool. Those become specialisation variants of one job (§7.2), not new jobs.
7. **Label.** One element → ADOPTED. Two or more → CLUSTERED, with the elements named. Zero → DERIVED with a stated inference, capped at 20% and listed in §5.5.

### 6.2 Worked example — CLUSTERED (B1, "Diagnose a failing test with no obvious cause")

**Inputs.** (a) LaToza _Debugging_ category, 26 reports, including "How did this runtime state occur?" (12) and "In what circumstances does this bug occur?" (3) [S10]. (b) O*NET 21670, "Modify existing software to correct errors, adapt it to new hardware, or upgrade interfaces and improve performance" [S1]. (c) O*NET 14661, "Perform initial debugging procedures by reviewing configuration files, logs, or code pieces to determine breakdown source" [S1]. (d) ISTQB test execution: "anomalies are analysed to identify their likely causes" [S6]. (e) Sonar "Debugging code (for example, stack trace analysis)", 65% adoption, 44% effectiveness [S13].

**Merge rationale.** All five share trigger class _observed unexpected behaviour against an expected check_ and done-artefact kind _causal claim with evidence + next action_. (b) and (c) split the work into "fix" and "find"; they are one job because the fix is not credible without the cause, and the cause is not useful without a decision — which is why the done column admits "fix **or** a narrow, named escalation".

**Splits this job is separated from, and why:**

- **C4 (trace a failure path)** — same code, different done kind: a path description, no cause, no fix. Trigger is a _question_, not a failure.
- **B8 (find the introducing change)** — trigger is a version delta, done is a commit identity.
- **T5 (prove a fix works)** — trigger is a closed defect, done is a test that fails on old code.
- **B3 (characterise an intermittent failure)** — split because the first required action differs: reproduce-rate measurement, not cause analysis, and the done condition admits "documented non-determinism".

### 6.3 Worked example — DERIVED (I6, "Ship a change behind a switch")

**Why nothing in the pool produces it.** ISO 12207 has no flag process [S4]; SWEBOK KA4 covers construction technologies but not release mechanics [S3]; O\*NET's software tasks contain no deployment-reversibility statement [S1]; the question studies are pre-continuous-delivery [S10]; the survey's items are task-level, not mechanism-level [S13].

**The inference chain.** DORA's continuous-delivery capability is defined as "deploying software … reliably, low-risk, on demand" [S14]. Low-risk on-demand deployment in a system without instantaneous rollback requires a way to disable a change without redeploying. Therefore the _job_ "make this change reversible in production" exists even though no taxonomy names the flag.

**Why it is needed rather than nice.** The catalogue already ships `feature-flag-orchestrator` and a release-governance mode, and the target state's preload includes both the concept and its owner. Without a job, those slugs have nothing to point at and §8.4 would classify them as jobless.

**Where it would go if rejected.** Fold into O2 (recover a failed deployment) and O3 (cut a release). Consequence: the preload loses one slot, and reversibility becomes a property nobody is accountable for until an incident.

### 7.3 C — what would populate it

`C` stays blank until evals exist. The protocol that would fill it, per job:

| Property     | Rule                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unit         | One scenario per job, written as `trigger → expected done-evidence`, with one deliberately plausible wrong answer                             |
| Failure mode | Every scenario names the failure it discriminates (e.g., B1: "plausible cause asserted without evidence")                                     |
| Pass         | The named evidence appears and the named failure does not                                                                                     |
| Axis value   | `C_axis = jobs with a passing scenario / jobs with a scenario`, published as a band: `none (0) / partial (<50%) / most (50–90%) / all (>90%)` |
| Anti-vanity  | A job with no scenario is counted as **uncovered**, never excluded from the denominator                                                       |
| Prohibited   | Do not infer `C` from mode presence, mode length, or catalogue description. Those are supply, and supply is already column A                  |

Consequence: `C` is a _coverage_ number, not a quality score, and it is comparable across axes only because the unit (job-scenario) is the same everywhere.

**Banding rule (corroboration, not a weighted average).**

- **deep** = at least **two** of: N₁ ≥ 10%, N₂ ≥ 8%, N₃ ≥ 75%, N₄ ≥ 50%.

- **thin** = any instrument above zero but neither test met; **none** = zero on all.

Excluding them is what makes N₁ a coding-work denominator rather than an employment denominator. **Vintage warning:** N₁ mixes 2025 ratings (software developers), 2022 (web), 2018 (programmers) and 2017 (QA). Because QA carries 27 of the 88 tasks, TST's 22.1% is the least contemporary number in the table; §2.4 item 8 states the consequence.

**Where N₁ and N₂ disagree, that is the finding.** Testing is the largest assigned task class (22.1%) and a small friction class (5.4%); comprehension is the reverse (0% / 49.1%). Both are true. A catalogue built from job descriptions would be a test-and-ops catalogue; one built from questions is a comprehension-and-debugging catalogue. The current preload is neither: it is 24.4% implementation slots and 0% comprehension/debugging.

### 7.5 Rules that keep the instrument honest

1. **Count jobs, not modes.** A mode is an owner; a job is a need. Publishing "302 modes" is a supply fact.
2. **Coarse bins before percentages.** Axis values are `none / thin / solid / deep`; percentages appear only where the denominator is ≥8 jobs or ≥80 rated tasks, and are labelled by source.
3. **C is blank, not estimated.** No eval, no number.
4. **N comes from outside.** No house category contributes to N; where a source cannot support an axis (UND in O\*NET), the source is reported as blind, not as zero demand.
5. **Never weight by usage.** No telemetry exists; a usage-weighted N would be fabrication.
6. **A job with no owner is a gap, not a bug.** It may justify `create`, or it may justify `skill` — decided in J3, not here.
7. **A mode with no job is a question, not a verdict.** It may be a specialisation, a duplicate, a product offering outside coding work, or dead weight. §8.4 classifies; ADR-6 decides.
8. **Publish the four quantities separately.** A single "coverage %" is prohibited; the defensible sentence is in [`modes-doctrine.md`](../modes-doctrine.md:307).

### 8.5 What would falsify this baseline

- A job inventory under 80 (axes too coarse) or over 160 (jobs are steps) — this one is at 119, which is within target; a reviewer who splits 40 more jobs is saying the axes are too coarse, and that is a legitimate finding.
- A source that shows the comprehension axis is _not_ separately served (e.g., that code explanation is fully absorbed by implementation modes) — that would collapse UND into IMP and remove the report's strongest recommendation.
- Evidence that preloaded specialisation variants materially improve routing or outcomes (i.e., that a stack variant is not a relabelling) — that would justify the current implementation-heavy preload and weaken §8.2.
- An eval that shows the 25 non-coding slots do get selected in coding workflows — that would make META/OUT supply evidence rather than surplus.
